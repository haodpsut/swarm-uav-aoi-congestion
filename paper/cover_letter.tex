\noindent
Dear Editor, dear Dr.~Tsai,

We are submitting the second revision of \textbf{IoT-70170-2026},
``Congestion-Aware Joint Charging-Station Placement, Capacity, and Swarm-UAV
Trajectory Design for Age-Optimal Data Collection''. The title, the problem, and the
contributions are unchanged.

\textbf{We must begin with an apology.} Two reviewers reported that our previous
response letter did not answer their comments. They were right, and the cause was
ours. The first-round decision letter carried five reviewers' comments in the body of
the e-mail and one reviewer's comments as an attachment. Only the attachment reached
our working copy, so the previous letter answered seven points while the reviewers had
raised forty-six. Nothing was declined and nothing was judged unimportant; the
material was never collected. The accompanying response letter now answers
\textbf{every numbered point of every reviewer from both rounds}: forty-six from the
first round and eight from the second, fifty-four in total. It is generated from a
ledger with one row per comment, so its completeness can be checked by counting rather
than taken on trust. Because the reviewer numbering changed between rounds, every old
point is numbered by its \emph{first-round} reviewer and both numbers appear in each
heading.

Three changes alter what the paper claims, and we list them here rather than leave
them to be found:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$M{=}12$ is no longer described as the optimal swarm size.} A reviewer
asked us to re-check that claim under the actual placement and port split, and the
check contradicted it: the table we had cited gives $M^\star{=}12$ for one station with
four ports, whereas the setup splits four ports over two stations. Sweeping $M$ under
the real configuration gives $M^\star{=}\rMstarOwn$. We keep $M{=}12$ as a fixed and
neutral operating point, note that it costs the proposed method $\rMstarPropCost\%$,
and repeat the entire comparison with every method at its own optimum, where the
advantages are essentially unchanged.

\item \textbf{The feasibility claim held only at the smallest field size.} While
answering a request to report the violation of every constraint, we found that our own
statement that the returned designs are ``feasible with margin to spare'' was measured
at a $5$~km field, whereas the main comparison runs at $15$~km, where the
recharge-reachability constraint is in fact relaxed. The paper now reports the field
sizes at which that constraint can and cannot be met, states that the large-field
results hold under an idealized-reachability assumption, and turns the finding into a
provisioning guideline. No reviewer raised this; we found it and we report it.

\item \textbf{The claims about the queueing model are narrower.} The agreement between
the analytic model and the simulator under matched assumptions is now described as a
sanity check of the implementation rather than as validation of the model, and the
``conservative upper bound'' language is replaced by an empirical overestimate on the
configurations tested. To answer the underlying concern we applied one \emph{common}
near-deterministic simulator to the final design of all five methods: the ordering is
identical to the analytic ordering and the proposed design wins on $\rDesPropWins$ of
$\rSeeds$ seeds.
\end{itemize}

Two procedural notes. First, all results in this version were regenerated on a single
documented machine, re-run from the exact commit that was submitted last time; of
$77$ previously reported quantities, $69$ are bit-identical and eight moved in the
third significant digit, with no conclusion changed. Second, the figures were redrawn
at the true column width after we measured that several were being scaled down on
placement, leaving text as small as $6.5$~pt; all figures now print at
$9.3$~pt or larger.

All changes are marked in the accompanying highlighted PDF, in blue for added text and
dark red for deleted text. \markupNote

Every reported quantity is generated from the artifact by script, and a single command
reproduces the measurements, recording a SHA-256 digest per result file so a rerun can
be checked rather than trusted:
\url{https://github.com/haodpsut/swarm-uav-aoi-congestion}.

We are grateful to the reviewers for their patience, and particularly to those who
told us plainly that their comments had gone unanswered.
