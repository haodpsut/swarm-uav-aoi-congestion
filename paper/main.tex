\begin{abstract}
Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms keep Internet of Things data fresh, but
energy-limited UAVs must recharge at a few shared stations, a
contended resource. We jointly design station placement, port capacity, swarm
trajectory, and UAV-to-station assignment to minimize network peak Age of
Information (AoI). Our solver targets a relaxation: recharge reachability is
enforced only to a field side of $\rEnvFullKm$~km, inter-UAV separation never. The charging system is a closed population of only $M$ UAVs,
so it needs a finite-source (machine-repair) rather than open-arrival queue; a discrete-event simulation agrees within about $4\%$ and the
model overestimates the near-deterministic wait on every configuration tested;
the open queue overpredicts it by roughly $6\times$ and distorts the design. We prove a congestion threshold making peak AoI non-monotone in swarm size,
with a provisioning law linking swarm size to charging capacity, and a placement
inversion: the coverage-optimal placement is AoI-suboptimal under contention. Our block-coordinate solver uses a GPU close-enough
traveling-salesman optimizer. Results are reported by feasibility regime.
Where every returned design can be flown (field side up to $\rEnvFullKm$~km), the
method cuts peak AoI by $\rSingleGainMin\%$ against a strong single pooled-station baseline at
$M{=}12$ and four ports, and $\rFamCovWorst\%$ on the worst seed. At a $9$~km field with
three stations no prior-art baseline is feasible, so the remaining $\rNineGain\%$
gain is measured against our own ablation and quantifies that component, not
an advantage over prior art. Larger fields exceed the energy budget
and are reported as an idealized-reachability study.
\end{abstract}
